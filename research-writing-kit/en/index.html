Research Writing Kit

A method for writing research papers and grant proposals with an AI, plus a small toolkit that installs and customizes itself. You don't need to know AI or do any setup. Hand the folder to your Claude; it asks you a few questions, then builds the tools that fit you.

Get it on GitHub research-writing-kit

What it is

Not a template. A method.

This is not something you paste in. You own the ideas and the argument; the AI does the legwork: finds and verifies sources, builds the argument skeleton, writes the prose, then checks its own work. Five principles run through the whole thing:

  1. Skeleton firstNever write prose from a blank page. That squeezing is where "AI prose" comes from. Build the argument structure first, then write to it.
  2. No fabricated citations, everEvery reference is actually fetched and its support direction verified. Can't verify it? It's marked "unverified", not faked. Before delivery it also scans for retractions and for claims with no citation at all.
  3. Your voiceWhen you write in your native language, the prose matches your own habits instead of being sanded into generic AI prose. English drafts get a separate rhythm pass before delivery.
  4. Honesty built inEffect sizes with confidence intervals, not just p-values. Likert items are not treated as continuous. Before claiming an effect, the design is checked for whether it can answer the question at all.
  5. Data stays on your machineUnpublished drafts and raw research data are never uploaded. Online services only ever see bibliographic data such as DOIs and titles.

Getting started

Three steps, five minutes

  1. Get the folder. It's a public repository, no login needed. Clone it with git, or use Code → Download ZIP on the GitHub page. 
    git clone https://github.com/chenweichiang/research-writing-kit.git
  2. Open Claude Code inside that folder. It needs a paid Claude plan; a free claude.ai account won't work. 
    cd research-writing-kit
claude
  3. Paste one line and let it take over. It will ask what you write, in which language, where you submit, and whether you have past writing it can learn your voice from. Then it generates skills that are yours. "Read CLAUDE.md and set me up."

Still on the claude.ai website with no Claude Code? Connect the repo to the web Claude and say the same line. It walks you onto Claude Code step by step (setup/WEB.md).

Day to day

Just say it

Open Claude Code in the folder that holds your paper and say what you need, the way you'd say it to a person. You can also type /skill-name directly.

"Help me write this paper / proposal"/co-author
Collaborative writing: sources, skeleton, prose, and six pre-delivery gates (retractions, uncited claims, numbers ledger, figure provenance, submission statements, final review).
"Check this draft before I submit"/paper-review
Five layers: mechanical, diction, language, reviewer's-eye logic, delivery completeness. Checks only; it does not rewrite.
"Do these citations really support what I wrote?"/verify-citations
Reads the cited PDFs sentence by sentence, judges support direction, verifies DOIs, and sends flagged items to a sceptical second reviewer.
"Get me the PDFs of these references"/fetch-refs
Multi-source fetching, per-paper content check, filing. Includes citation snowballing.
"The reviews are back, help me respond"/rebuttal
Split the points, rule on each, make the revisions, write the letter, verify completeness before sending.
"Don't make that mistake again"/doc-regress
Turns a caught error into a standing check that scans the whole document and blocks recurrence. Includes the numbers ledger.
"Build the submission PDF"/build-pdf
Formats to the venue template; second-language papers ship with the back-translation as a pair.

What's inside

Method, templates, sixteen tools

  • The method in four documents: philosophy, iron rules, the eight-phase workflow, argumentation moves.
  • Seven skill templates and two subagents: adapted to your field, language, and venues at install time, not copied verbatim.
  • Sixteen local scripts, most with zero install: Taiwan-usage and Chinese AI-style checks, English grammar and AI-fingerprint diagnostics, figure colour-vision accessibility, retraction scan, uncited-claims scan, document regression, response-letter and submission-ledger checks.
  • Blank templates (voice profile, venue notes, argument skeleton), open academic word lists (two bundled; the AWL is fetched by a script because of its licence), one worked skeleton example.
  • Traditional Chinese (Taiwan) add-on: mainland-vs-Taiwan term check, 台/臺 consistency, a Typst recipe for Chinese PDFs.

Everything in detail is in the repo's README.en.md: what appears on your computer after install, what grows inside your paper project, and which external program or online service each feature uses.
